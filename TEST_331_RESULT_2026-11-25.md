# FIELD 331 — RESULT OF THE INDOOR TEST WITH POWER, 2026-11-25

**Field 331 displays the DURATION OF THE MOST RECENTLY COMPLETED LAP
(which, in a structured workout, is the workout step).**

That is a positive identification of a quantity, not another
elimination. It is the first one this investigation has produced.

Predictions in `TEST_331_indoor_with_power.txt` were 12:30, 14:00 or
28:00 — a single accumulating value. **All three were wrong.** The
field is not an accumulator at all; it is a per-lap latch. Seventeen
readings instead of one is what exposed that, and the credit is Doug's
for taking a reading at every change instead of one at the end.

---

## The evidence

Workout from the activity file (`2026-11-25-09-55-38.fit`, label
`7x2:00@185W`, step name `VO2 Max`): warm-up 15:00, then 7 ×
(2:00 work, 1:00 recovery), then 15:00 cool-down. Sixteen laps, each
exactly matching its step — 900.0, then 120.0/60.0 alternating, then
900.0 s, with `elapsed == timer` on every one.

Each reading is placed against the lap that had just **closed** at that
moment:

Reading 7 is the single outlier: 01:56 where the closed lap was 1:00.
It is within 4 s of **2:00**, the lap before that, so the likely cause
is a glance taken a beat before the display caught up — but it is
recorded as an outlier rather than explained away.

## What this kills

- **Time in Target is dead**, and it had been the leading candidate.
  Average power fell well short of the step's target band in **every**
  work interval — in the worst case roughly half of it — so time
  actually spent in target was nowhere near the full two minutes. 331
  read exactly 2:00 after every one of them regardless. The field is
  indifferent to whether the target was met.
- **Every accumulating candidate is dead** by the same evidence: the
  value goes *down* as well as up, which no accumulator does.
- **Power was never the variable.** Power mattered only in so far as it
  was needed to run the session. This is a correction to Doc rev 227 and
  to the test plan built on it — see the open question below.

## What it does NOT yet settle: which lap-time variant

The toolkit already names **id 87 "Last Lap Time"**, taken from the
device's own label. So 331 is not simply that field; it is a variant or
a sibling of it. Candidates:

1. **Last lap elapsed vs last lap timer time.** Indistinguishable
   today — every lap had `elapsed == timer`, because nothing was paused
   on a trainer.
2. **Last *step* time rather than last *lap* time.** Identical inside a
   workout, because each step is a lap. They diverge the moment you
   press Lap outside a workout.
3. Something with the same value and a different intended consumer,
   which is consistent with 331 sitting in the unlabelled 840-only
   group alongside SOG and Battery-hours.

## Two cheap tests settle it

**TEST A — 87 beside 331, three minutes, any ride.** Put field 87 on
the same screen as 331 and ride two laps. If they always agree, 331 is
a duplicate of last-lap-time and the hunt is over. If they diverge, the
difference *is* the answer.

**TEST B — press Lap with NO workout loaded, three minutes.** Ride, hit
the Lap button, ride a bit, hit it again.

- 331 shows the lap length → it is **lap**-based.
- 331 stays 00:00 → it is **workout-step**-based, and the distinction
  matters because it would also explain the 2026-11-24 ride.

Run A and B in the same session and one trainer ride answers both.

## ⚠ Two things do not reconcile, and they are both mine, not Doug's

**1. The two outdoor values.** 14:20 and 13:41 were recorded as the
final 331 readings on the 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-17 rides. Both of those
workouts end with a **900 s cool-down step**, so under this reading both
should have shown exactly 15:00. They are 40 s and 79 s short. Today's
equivalent reading was 3 s short. The direction is consistent across all
three rides — always short, never over — and so is the sign of the small
deviations in the table above. The magnitude is not.

Those outdoor figures are single glances written down afterwards;
today's is a 17-point series taken at the moment of each change. When a
one-point recollection disagrees with a time series, the series wins —
but the discrepancy is logged rather than dismissed, because 40 and
79 seconds is too much to wave away as a mis-read.

**2. ~~The 2026-11-24 negative.~~ ✅ RESOLVED — see below.** It was
never a conflict, and my premise about that ride was wrong.

# ADDENDUM — the 2026-11-24 file, same day

Doug sent the file instead of re-riding. Right call: it answers from
the record what neither of us could answer from memory.

**It was not the workout I thought.** The file says workout `90W`, step
name `Base`, **one single step of 53:00**, producing two laps — the
53:00 step, then a 29 s free-ride lap. `2x14x0:10@290W` was the 11-17
*outdoor* ride; I had conflated them. The power-free condition is
confirmed from the file: **no power field in any of its 3,210 record
messages**, and `avg_power`, `max_power` and `total_work` are all the
FIT "invalid" value.

## (a) The 00:00 is fully explained

A single-step workout closes exactly one lap, at the very end. So 331
*correctly* showed 00:00 for all 53 minutes — there was no completed
lap to report. The only window where it could have shown anything was
the 29 seconds after the completion signal, while Doug was stopping.
Power was never involved in any of it.

## (b) It kills the rival reading of today's data — the important part

The alternative to a latch was a **running** current-lap timer: plain
"Lap Time" counting up, caught just before each reset. That reading was
genuinely attractive, for three reasons:

- it explains the systematic 1-to-4-second shortfalls in the table
  above, which a latch should never produce;
- it explains the outdoor 14:20 and 13:41 as glances taken part-way
  into a 15:00 cool-down;
- **no plain "Lap Time" or "Timer" appears anywhere in the 213 named
  ids** — conspicuous, for a field every Edge has.

A running lap timer would have climbed visibly from 00:00 to 53:00
across yesterday's single 53-minute lap. Doug watched it sit at 00:00.
So **331 is latched, not running.** Two live hypotheses, one file, one
survivor — and it cost one upload instead of a ride.

This also means **TEST B is now a tidy-up rather than the priority.**
TEST A — 87 beside 331 — is the one that still earns its keep.

## What is left open

The outdoor 14:20 and 13:41, where both rides' final closed lap was
900 s. Both are single glances recorded afterwards, against a 17-point
series and a 53-minute null. They are logged as an unexplained residual,
not treated as counter-evidence — and not explained either.

## Method note, for the next investigation

Nine candidates were eliminated one at a time over several weeks from
single end-of-ride readings. The identification came from one ride with
seventeen timestamped readings. The lesson is not "test more often" in
the abstract — it is that **a latched value is invisible to end-of-ride
sampling**, and nothing in the first nine data points could have
distinguished a latch from an accumulator. Sample at transitions, not at
the end.
